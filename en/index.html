How it works

Higgsfield is a service that brings different neural networks together: some make images, some make video, some make sound. Here they are called models.

A video is almost always built in three steps.

  1. Images

    The character, the location, the object: whatever has to look the same in every frame.

  2. Video

    The model brings the scene to life from your description and those images.

  3. Editing

    Cuts, color, titles. That part happens in CapCut or DaVinci Resolve.

For one simple scene you can skip the first step and go straight to the text.

Five terms you will see below

Prompt
A text description of the scene for the model.
Shot
One continuous piece of the video, from one cut to the next.
HARD CUT
A sharp cut: one shot is replaced by the next at once.
Credits
The service's internal money. Every generation spends some.
480p, 720p, 1080p
Image sharpness. The higher the number, the sharper and the more expensive.

03

Character — location — object

You can give the model not only text but also reference images: the character, the location, the product. In Higgsfield they are called References. The model sticks to the reference, and the character stays the same from video to video.

When do you need a reference? One question answers it:

Will it appear more than once?

One scene, a nameless character
Prepare nothing. Describe the character in words in the prompt.
One character in several videos
Make a reference image of the character first and save it.
An ad: the product exactly as it is
Upload a photo of the product. Don't describe it in words.
One location, several shots
Upload an image of the location.
Exact colors and style matter
Upload a style reference image.

How to make a character reference

  1. Open the Image section

    It is at the top, next to Video. Pick an image model from section 2.

  2. Describe the character

    Age, face, hair, build, clothes. Ask for a character sheet on a plain gray background: the face from the front and a full-body view.

  3. Download the image

    Upload it to References in every video with this character.

The order

First, everything that repeats: the character, their clothes, the location, the objects. Then the video. If you start with the video, the character will be a new person in every clip.

This is the window where everything happens. The numbers on the screenshot are the step numbers.

  1. Open the Video section

    Go to higgsfield.ai, sign up or log in, and click Video at the top. The Create Video tab opens on the left.

  2. Pick the model

    Find the Model row under the text field, click it and choose Seedance 2.5.

  3. Upload references

    If you have references for the character, location or product: Add references. You can put an image, a video or a sound here. Do it before the text.

  4. Paste the prompt

    Your text goes into the Prompt field. How to write it is in sections 5 and 6.

  5. Leave the sound on

    The On button under the field is the sound. The model makes it by itself, together with the video.

  6. Length, aspect ratio, quality

    Three buttons in a row. 9:16 is for Reels and TikTok, 16:9 is for YouTube.

  7. Look at the price

    The Generate button shows how many credits (the service's internal money) will be spent. The number changes with length and quality.

  8. Click Generate

    In a couple of minutes the finished video appears on the right, in History. Download it. If the prompt had several shots, the video is already cut together.

Draft first

For a test, set a lower quality or choose Seedance 2.5 Draft in the model list. It comes out noticeably cheaper; check the price on the Generate button. When everything is in place, run the same prompt in good quality.

What else is in the window

The Rerun button under a video in History runs the same prompt again. The Extend Video tab makes a finished video longer.

05

How a prompt works

The model needs a list: what, who, where, how it is shot. It understands a story told "from start to finish" worse.

A prompt is one text where each part starts with a label. This order is recommended by Higgsfield itself:

GLOBAL STYLE
Genre, color, film or digital look, aspect ratio, and what must not be there.
SCENE
One line: what happens, where, what mood.
CHARACTERS
Each person: face, hair, build, clothes.
LOCATION
The place and the objects in it, separately from the people.
FIRST FRAME AND BLOCKING
Who stands where and looks where in the first frame.
SHOT 1, SHOT 2…
Each shot (a piece of the video from cut to cut): framing and action, by the second.
OPTICS
The lens for each shot.
CAMERA
The camera movement for each shot.
PHYSICS
How fabric, hair, smoke and water move.
LIGHTING
Where the light comes from and what kind it is.
AUDIO
What is heard and what must not be heard.

Skip a part and the model fills it in by itself, usually the wrong way. Without LOCATION, for example, the place changes from shot to shot.

If you uploaded references

Don't describe the character in words again. Say what to take from the image: identity and wardrobe locked to the reference image, ignore the reference backdrop. The text then describes only the action and the camera.

A simple version for the first time

One or two sentences in this order: who is doing what → where and in what light → how the camera moves → the mood. Sounds go separately.

Example
A woman walks through a crowded train station at rush hour, checking her phone, camera tracking close at shoulder height, warm overhead fluorescent light, tense and rushed. SFX: station ambient noise, announcements in the background.
06

Prompt with a chat assistant

You don't have to write the prompt from scratch. Describe the scene in your own words, and a chat assistant turns it into a prompt: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or any other.

On a free plan

In a free chat you usually have to give the rules again in every conversation, and the number of messages is limited. So send everything in one message. Copy it and add your scene at the end.

Message to the assistant
You write prompts for the Seedance 2.5 video model.

Rules:
1. The answer is one text, and each section starts with a label. Order: GLOBAL STYLE, SCENE, CHARACTERS, LOCATION, FIRST FRAME AND BLOCKING, SHOT 1…N (with seconds), OPTICS, CAMERA, PHYSICS, LIGHTING, AUDIO.
2. One action per shot, and it is already in progress in the first frame.
3. Name objects in plain words: smartphone, glass bottle.
4. Each shot has its own camera movement. The camera is never completely still.
5. Shots have different lengths. At the end of every shot except the last one, write HARD CUT (a sharp cut).
6. Light: where it comes from and what kind it is. Not "beautiful light".
7. At the end of GLOBAL STYLE, the bans: no text on screen, no subtitles, no logos, no slow motion.
8. Faces are invented: every face is fully original and invented.

First ask me up to three questions if something is missing. Then write.

My scene: [describe: who, what they are doing, where, how many seconds, vertical or horizontal]

After that, ask for one change at a time: "in shot 2 replace the camera movement with a slow push-in, leave the rest as it is".

On a paid plan

What exactly is available depends on the service and the plan. Check in your own chat.

  • Save the rules once

    Create a project (that is what it is called in ChatGPT and Claude) and put the rules from the block above into it. In new conversations inside the project they already apply.

  • Show an image

    Attach a reference frame or an image of the character and ask: "describe the character and the light so that the model can repeat them". An assistant describes from an image more precisely than a person does from memory.

  • Break down a failure

    Attach a still from the video you got and the prompt: "this is what I asked for, this is what came out. What is wrong in the prompt? Change one thing".

  • Keep a log

    Store the prompts that worked in the same project. Ask for the next video "like the one that worked, but with a different character and location".

A free assistant will also write a prompt by the rules, and many free versions read images too. A paid one is more convenient because it remembers the rules and cuts the conversation off in the middle less often.

How to get realism

By default the model makes a glossy picture. To make it look like real footage, ask for imperfections directly. Paste the lines below into your prompt as they are.

Skin and face

In CHARACTERS
skin unretouched and hyper-detailed: pores, fine lines, slight asymmetry. Natural pore texture, no over-retouching, no beauty filter.

Add small living details: natural blinks, a reaction that comes half a second late.

Light

One real source and its direction.

In LIGHTING
one real light source: high midday sun from the left, hard shadows

It also helps to say what you don't want: NOT soft, NOT diffused.

Camera

Say who is shooting and how. Handheld:

In CAMERA
handheld weight, operator breathing, small human corrections, no smooth drone glides

On a tripod or a dolly:

In CAMERA
slow, deliberate, machine-smooth dolly move, no handheld jitter

For a video that looks shot on a phone: arm's-length wobble, tiny refocus moments.

Lens and film

Set one lens per shot, for example 35mm lens, and don't change it inside the shot. For a cinematic look, add grain:

In GLOBAL STYLE
light authentic 35mm film grain, halation bloom on highlights

Motion and weight

The main giveaway of AI video is that everything "floats". Ask for weight:

In PHYSICS
walking carries true weight: heel strike, weight transfer. Hair strands move and release, never static. Fabric lags behind the body.

And always: real-time motion, no slow motion.

Sound

Only the sounds of the place:

In AUDIO
on-location sound only: footsteps on wet pavement, distant traffic. No music, no narration.

What else helps

  • One action per shot

    Three actions in three seconds turn into a mess.

  • Keep the character big

    The character and the main object take up at least half of the frame. Small things are drawn worse.

  • Add a second person

    If the scene allows it: a passer-by, a seller. A frame where someone reacts to the character looks more alive.

  • Nobody looks at the camera

    Unless it is a talk to the viewer: nobody looks into the lens.

  • Don't ask for text in the frame

    It comes out as gibberish. Add captions in editing.

  • Finish the color in editing

    If the model didn't give you the grain or color you want in two tries, don't spend credits. Add them in editing.

08

If it came out wrong

  1. Change one thing at a time

    Either the prompt or the reference, not both. Otherwise you won't know what helped.

  2. One bad shot? Fix only that shot

    Change the description of that shot and run again. Leave the rest of the text alone.

  3. Two to four tries is normal

    That is how many one shot usually takes.

  4. Don't argue with the model

    If it keeps failing at an exact hand pose or a crowd that reacts on cue, think of a different shot and save your credits.
